Physics Formula Reference
Formulas with worked examples · g = 9.8 m/s² unless stated

Projectile Motion

Time to maximum height
t = U sinθg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A ball is projected at 20 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Find the time to reach maximum height.
t = (20 sin 30°) / 9.8 = (20 × 0.5) / 9.8 = 1.02 s
Total time of flight
T = 2U sinθg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A stone is thrown at 25 m/s at 40° to the horizontal. Find the total time of flight.
T = (2 × 25 × sin 40°) / 9.8 = (50 × 0.643) / 9.8 = 3.28 s
Maximum height
H = U² sin²θ2g
WORKED EXAMPLE
A ball is kicked at 15 m/s at 50° to the horizontal. Find the maximum height reached.
H = (15² × sin²50°) / (2 × 9.8) = (225 × 0.587) / 19.6 = 6.74 m
Range
R = U² sin 2θg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A stone is projected at 30 m/s at 25° to the horizontal. Find the horizontal range.
R = (30² × sin 50°) / 9.8 = (900 × 0.766) / 9.8 = 70.35 m
Maximum range (θ = 45°)
Rmax = U²g
WORKED EXAMPLE
A ball is projected at 25 m/s. What launch angle gives the greatest range, and what is that range?
Angle = 45°. Rmax = 25² / 9.8 = 625 / 9.8 = 63.78 m
Horizontal projectile — time to fall
t = √2hg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A ball rolls off a table 1.8 m high. How long does it take to reach the floor?
t = √(2 × 1.8 / 9.8) = √0.367 = 0.61 s
Horizontal projectile — horizontal distance
S = ux t
WORKED EXAMPLE
A marble rolls off a bench 1.6 m high at 4 m/s. Find the horizontal distance travelled before it hits the floor.
t = √(2×1.6/9.8) = 0.572 s. S = 4 × 0.572 = 2.29 m

Motion — Constant Acceleration

Final velocity
v = u + at
WORKED EXAMPLE
A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 5 s. Find its final velocity.
v = 0 + 3(5) = 15 m/s
Distance
s = ut + 12 at²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A train moving at 10 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s² for 8 s. Find the distance covered.
s = 10(8) + ½(2)(8²) = 80 + 64 = 144 m
Velocity without time
v² = u² + 2as
WORKED EXAMPLE
A car at 20 m/s brakes to rest over 40 m. Find the deceleration.
0 = 20² + 2a(40) → 0 = 400 + 80a → a = −5 m/s²
Average velocity
vavg = ΔxΔt
WORKED EXAMPLE
A cyclist covers 240 m in 30 s. Find the average velocity.
vavg = 240 / 30 = 8 m/s
Average acceleration
aavg = ΔvΔt
WORKED EXAMPLE
An object's speed rises from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s. Find its average acceleration.
aavg = (25 − 5) / 4 = 5 m/s²
Free fall (downward)
v = u + gt    h = ut + 12 gt²    v² = u² + 2gh
WORKED EXAMPLE
A stone is dropped from a tower 45 m tall. Find its velocity just before hitting the ground.
v² = 0 + 2(9.8)(45) = 882 → v = 29.7 m/s
Thrown upward
v = u − gt    h = ut − 12 gt²    v² = u² − 2gh
WORKED EXAMPLE
A ball is thrown vertically upward at 30 m/s. Find the maximum height reached.
At max height v = 0: 0 = 30² − 2(9.8)h → h = 900 / 19.6 = 45.9 m

Vectors

Magnitude
|A| = √(Ax² + Ay² + Az²)
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the magnitude of vector A = (3, 4, 12).
|A| = √(9 + 16 + 144) = √169 = 13
Dot product
A · B = AB cosθ = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz
WORKED EXAMPLE
Two vectors of magnitudes 5 and 3 make an angle of 60° with each other. Find A · B.
A · B = 5 × 3 × cos 60° = 15 × 0.5 = 7.5
Cross product magnitude
|A × B| = AB sinθ
WORKED EXAMPLE
Vectors A (magnitude 5) and B (magnitude 3) are perpendicular. Find |A × B|.
|A × B| = 5 × 3 × sin 90° = 15
Work done by a vector force
W = F · d
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 10 N pulls a box 5 m along the floor, at 60° to the horizontal. Find the work done.
W = 10 × 5 × cos 60° = 25 J
Angle between two vectors
cosθ = A · BAB
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the angle between A = (1, 2, 3) and B = (4, 5, 6).
A·B = 32, |A| = √14, |B| = √77. cosθ = 32 / √1078 ≈ 0.975 → θ ≈ 12.9°

Forces and Newton's Laws

Net force
Fnet = ma
WORKED EXAMPLE
A net force of 40 N acts on a 10 kg body. Find its acceleration.
a = F / m = 40 / 10 = 4 m/s²
Weight
W = mg
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the weight of a 5 kg object on Earth.
W = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N
Maximum static friction
fs = μsN
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 20 kg block rests on a horizontal floor (μs = 0.4). Find the maximum static friction.
N = 20 × 9.8 = 196 N. fs = 0.4 × 196 = 78.4 N
Kinetic friction
fk = μkN
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 30 kg crate slides along a floor with μk = 0.25. Find the friction opposing motion.
N = 30 × 9.8 = 294 N. fk = 0.25 × 294 = 73.5 N
Equilibrium
ΣF = 0    Στ = 0
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 5 kg book rests on a table. What is the normal reaction from the table?
ΣF = 0 → N = W = mg = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N
Torque (moment of a force)
τ = Fr
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 50 N is applied perpendicular to a spanner at 0.2 m from the nut. Find the torque.
τ = 50 × 0.2 = 10 N·m
Centre of mass
xcm = Σ mixiΣ mi
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 2 kg mass sits at x = 0 and a 3 kg mass at x = 10 m. Find the centre of mass.
xcm = (2×0 + 3×10) / (2+3) = 30 / 5 = 6 m

Momentum and Collisions

Momentum
p = mv
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the momentum of a 4 kg object moving at 5 m/s.
p = 4 × 5 = 20 kg·m/s
Impulse
I = Ft = Δp
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.5 kg ball speeds up from 10 m/s to 20 m/s. Find the impulse delivered.
I = Δp = 0.5(20 − 10) = 5 N·s
Conservation of momentum
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s hits a stationary 3 kg ball. After impact the 2 kg ball rebounds at 1 m/s. Find the 3 kg ball's velocity.
2(5) + 3(0) = 2(−1) + 3v → 10 = −2 + 3v → v = 4 m/s
Perfectly inelastic collision
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 2 kg ball at 5 m/s hits a stationary 3 kg ball and they stick together. Find their common velocity.
2(5) + 0 = (2 + 3)v → 10 = 5v → v = 2 m/s
Explosion / recoil
m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 4 kg shell at rest explodes into two parts. The 3 kg part moves at 4 m/s. Find the velocity of the 1 kg part.
0 = 3(4) + 1(v) → v = −12 m/s (opposite direction, speed 12 m/s)

Gravitation

Newton's law of gravitation
F = G m₁ m₂r²
WORKED EXAMPLE
Two 10 kg masses are 2 m apart. Find the gravitational attraction between them.
F = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 10 × 10) / 2² = 1.67 × 10⁻⁹ N
Acceleration due to gravity on a planet
g = GMR²
WORKED EXAMPLE
Calculate g at the Earth's surface (M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m).
g = (6.67e-11 × 5.97e24) / (6.4e6)² ≈ 9.7 m/s²
Gravitational potential
V = − GMr
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the gravitational potential at the Earth's surface.
V = −(6.67e-11 × 5.97e24) / 6.4e6 ≈ −6.2 × 10⁷ J/kg
Escape velocity
ve = √2GMR = √(2gR)
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the escape velocity from Earth's surface.
ve = √(2 × 9.8 × 6.4e6) = √(1.254e8) ≈ 11.2 km/s
Kepler's third law
T² ∝ r³    T² = 4π² r³GM
WORKED EXAMPLE
A satellite orbits at twice the Earth's radius from the centre. Find the ratio of its period to that of a satellite at the Earth's surface.
T² ∝ r³ → T₂/T₁ = (2R/R)3/2 = 2.83

Work, Energy and Power

Work done
W = FS cosθ
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 20 N moves a body 5 m, at 60° to the direction of motion. Find the work done.
W = 20 × 5 × cos 60° = 50 J
Kinetic energy
K = 12 mv²
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the kinetic energy of a 2 kg object moving at 4 m/s.
K = ½(2)(4²) = 16 J
Gravitational potential energy
PE = mgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 5 kg box is raised 3 m. Find the gain in gravitational potential energy.
PE = 5 × 9.8 × 3 = 147 J
Elastic potential energy (spring)
PE = 12 kx²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A spring of constant 200 N/m is compressed 0.1 m. Find its stored energy.
PE = ½(200)(0.1²) = 1 J
Work–energy theorem
W = ΔK
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 2 kg object accelerates from rest to 5 m/s. Find the net work done on it.
W = ΔK = ½(2)(5²) − 0 = 25 J
Power
P = Wt = Fv
WORKED EXAMPLE
A car engine applies a constant force of 800 N while moving at 25 m/s. Find the power output.
P = Fv = 800 × 25 = 20,000 W (20 kW)

Circular and Rotational Motion

Centripetal acceleration
ac = v²r = rω²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A car moves at 20 m/s on a curve of radius 50 m. Find the centripetal acceleration.
ac = 20² / 50 = 8 m/s²
Centripetal force
Fc = m v²r = mrω²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 1200 kg car rounds a curve of radius 80 m at 25 m/s. Find the centripetal force needed.
Fc = (1200 × 25²) / 80 = 750,000 / 80 = 9,375 N
Angular velocity
ω = vr = 2πT = 2πf
WORKED EXAMPLE
A wheel of radius 0.5 m spins so a point on its rim moves at 10 m/s. Find its angular velocity.
ω = 10 / 0.5 = 20 rad/s
Rotational torque
τ = Iα
WORKED EXAMPLE
A flywheel of moment of inertia 2 kg·m² accelerates at 4 rad/s². Find the torque applied.
τ = 2 × 4 = 8 N·m
Moment of inertia — solid disc
I = 12 MR²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A disc of mass 10 kg and radius 0.5 m rotates about its centre. Find its moment of inertia.
I = ½(10)(0.5²) = 1.25 kg·m²
Moment of inertia — solid sphere
I = 25 MR²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A solid sphere of mass 5 kg and radius 0.2 m rotates about a diameter. Find I.
I = (2/5)(5)(0.2²) = (2/5)(5)(0.04) = 0.08 kg·m²
Moment of inertia — rod about centre
I = 112 ML²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A uniform rod of mass 3 kg and length 2 m rotates about its centre. Find I.
I = (1/12)(3)(2²) = (1/12)(3)(4) = 1 kg·m²
Moment of inertia — rod about one end
I = 13 ML²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A uniform rod of mass 4 kg and length 3 m rotates about one end. Find I.
I = (1/3)(4)(3²) = (1/3)(4)(9) = 12 kg·m²
Angular momentum
L = Iω
WORKED EXAMPLE
A disc of moment of inertia 0.5 kg·m² spins at 8 rad/s. Find its angular momentum.
L = 0.5 × 8 = 4 kg·m²/s
Rotational kinetic energy
KErot = 12 Iω²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A flywheel of moment of inertia 3 kg·m² spins at 10 rad/s. Find its rotational kinetic energy.
KErot = ½(3)(10²) = 150 J
Rolling body total KE
KE = 12 mv² + 12 Iω²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A solid cylinder (M = 2 kg, R = 0.1 m) rolls along the ground at 3 m/s. Find its total kinetic energy.
I = ½MR² = 0.01 kg·m², ω = v/R = 30 rad/s. KE = ½(2)(9) + ½(0.01)(900) = 9 + 4.5 = 13.5 J
Parallel axis theorem
I = Icm + Mh²
WORKED EXAMPLE
Derive the moment of inertia of a uniform rod (mass M, length L) about one end, using the theorem.
I = 1/12 ML² + M(L/2)² = (1/12 + 1/4)ML² = 1/3 ML²
Perpendicular axis theorem
Iz = Ix + Iy
WORKED EXAMPLE
A plane lamina has Ix = 3 kg·m² and Iy = 4 kg·m². Find Iz about the axis through the centre perpendicular to the plane.
Iz = 3 + 4 = 7 kg·m²

Simple Harmonic Motion

Spring–mass period
T = 2π √mk
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.5 kg mass hangs from a spring of stiffness 50 N/m. Find the period of oscillation.
T = 2π√(0.5/50) = 2π√0.01 = 0.63 s
Simple pendulum period
T = 2π √Lg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A simple pendulum of length 1 m swings near the Earth's surface. Find its period.
T = 2π√(1/9.8) ≈ 2.0 s
Frequency and angular frequency
f = 1T    ω = 2πf
WORKED EXAMPLE
A pendulum completes one full swing in 2.5 s. Find its frequency and angular frequency.
f = 1 / 2.5 = 0.4 Hz, ω = 2π(0.4) = 2.51 rad/s
SHM displacement
x = A cos(ωt + φ)
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body oscillates with A = 0.1 m, ω = π rad/s, φ = 0. Find its displacement at t = 0.5 s.
x = 0.1 cos(π × 0.5) = 0.1 × 0 = 0 m
SHM velocity
v = −Aω sin(ωt + φ)
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body in SHM has amplitude 0.15 m, angular frequency 2 rad/s, φ = 0. Find its velocity at t = 0.4 s.
v = −0.15 × 2 × sin(0.8) = −0.3 × 0.717 = −0.215 m/s
SHM acceleration
a = −ω²x
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body in SHM is displaced 0.05 m with ω = 4 rad/s. Find its acceleration.
a = −(4²)(0.05) = −0.8 m/s²
Total energy in SHM
E = 12 kA²
WORKED EXAMPLE
A mass oscillates on a spring of constant 100 N/m with amplitude 0.05 m. Find its total energy.
E = ½(100)(0.05²) = ½(100)(0.0025) = 0.125 J
