Elasticity & Solids

Hooke's law
F = Ke
WORKED EXAMPLE
A spring stretches 0.05 m when a 10 N force is applied. Find the force when the same spring stretches 0.12 m.
K = 10 / 0.05 = 200 N/m. F = 200 × 0.12 = 24 N
Spring constant (stiffness)
K = Fe
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 24 N load produces a 6 cm extension in a spring. Find its spring constant.
K = 24 / 0.06 = 400 N/m
Stress
σ = FA
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 500 N acts on a wire of cross-sectional area 4 × 10⁻³ m². Find the stress.
σ = 500 / 4×10⁻³ = 1.25 × 10⁵ Pa
Strain
ε = eL
WORKED EXAMPLE
A wire 2 m long stretches by 0.5 mm under a load. Find the strain.
ε = 0.0005 / 2 = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴
Young's modulus
E = stressstrain = FLAe
WORKED EXAMPLE
A steel wire 3 m long and 2 mm in diameter stretches by 1.2 mm under a 200 N load. Find Young's modulus.
A = π(0.001)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ m². E = (200 × 3) / (3.14 × 10⁻⁶ × 1.2 × 10⁻³) ≈ 1.59 × 10¹¹ Pa
Shear modulus
G = FAθ
WORKED EXAMPLE
A tangential force of 50 N acts on a face of area 0.01 m², producing a shear angle of 0.02 rad. Find G.
G = 50 / (0.01 × 0.02) = 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa
Bulk modulus
B = − ΔPΔV / V
WORKED EXAMPLE
A liquid is compressed by 0.1% of its volume under an applied pressure of 2 × 10⁵ Pa. Find the bulk modulus.
ΔV/V = −0.001. B = −(2 × 10⁵) / (−0.001) = 2 × 10⁸ Pa
Poisson's ratio
σ = − Δd / dΔL / L
WORKED EXAMPLE
A wire is stretched by 0.1%, causing its diameter to contract by 0.03%. Find Poisson's ratio.
σ = −(−0.0003) / 0.001 = 0.3
Work done in stretching a wire
W = 12 Fe
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 20 N stretches a wire by 4 cm. Find the work done in stretching it.
W = ½(20)(0.04) = 0.4 J

Density & Relative Density

Density
ρ = mV
WORKED EXAMPLE
A solid has a mass of 500 g and a volume of 2 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Find its density.
ρ = 0.5 / 2×10⁻⁴ = 2500 kg/m³
Relative density
RD = ρsubρwater
WORKED EXAMPLE
A substance has density 2700 kg/m³. Given that water is 1000 kg/m³, find its relative density.
RD = 2700 / 1000 = 2.7
RD from density bottle
RD = M₂ − M₁M₃ − M₁
WORKED EXAMPLE
An empty density bottle weighs 25 g. Filled with a liquid it weighs 75 g; filled with water it weighs 65 g. Find the liquid's RD.
RD = (75 − 25) / (65 − 25) = 50 / 40 = 1.25
RD from weight in air / water
RD = W₁W₁ − W₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body weighs 10 N in air and 6 N in water. Find its relative density.
RD = 10 / (10 − 6) = 2.5
Density of substance from RD
ρ = RD × 1000 kg/m³
WORKED EXAMPLE
A liquid has a relative density of 1.25. Find its actual density in kg/m³.
ρ = 1.25 × 1000 = 1250 kg/m³

Pressure in Fluids

Pressure
P = FA
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 200 N acts on a surface of area 0.5 m². Find the pressure.
P = 200 / 0.5 = 400 Pa
Pressure due to liquid column
P = ρgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the pressure at a depth of 5 m in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³).
P = 1000 × 9.8 × 5 = 49,000 Pa
Absolute pressure
P = Patm + ρgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the absolute pressure at 5 m depth in water (Patm = 101,300 Pa).
P = 101,300 + 49,000 = 150,300 Pa
Gauge pressure
Pg = Pabs − Patm
WORKED EXAMPLE
A tyre's absolute pressure reads 220,000 Pa. Find the gauge pressure (Patm = 101,300 Pa).
Pg = 220,000 − 101,300 = 118,700 Pa
Manometer pressure
P = Patm + ρgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
A mercury manometer shows a 15 cm height difference. Find the absolute pressure (ρHg = 13,600 kg/m³).
P = 101,300 + (13,600 × 9.8 × 0.15) = 101,300 + 19,992 = 121,292 Pa
Barometer (atmospheric pressure)
Patm = ρgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
A mercury barometer reads 760 mm of Hg. Find Patm in Pa.
Patm = 13,600 × 9.8 × 0.76 = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa
Standard atmosphere
1 atm = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa = 760 mmHg

Archimedes & Floatation

Upthrust from weights
FB = W₁ − W₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
An object weighs 12 N in air and 8 N when fully submerged in water. Find the upthrust.
FB = 12 − 8 = 4 N
Upthrust (Archimedes' principle)
FB = Vρg
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body of volume 4 × 10⁻⁴ m³ is fully immersed in water. Find the upthrust.
FB = 4×10⁻⁴ × 1000 × 9.8 = 3.92 N
Density from weights in air and water
ρ = W₁W₁ − W₂ ρwater
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body weighs 10 N in air and 6 N in water. Find its density.
ρ = (10 / 4) × 1000 = 2500 kg/m³
Principle of floatation
Weight of body = Upthrust
WORKED EXAMPLE
A block of wood floats with 60% of its volume submerged. Find its density (ρwater = 1000 kg/m³).
ρwood × V × g = 0.6V × 1000 × g → ρwood = 600 kg/m³
Hydrometer relation
ρ ∝ 1h
WORKED EXAMPLE
A hydrometer floats with 4 cm stem exposed in water and 6 cm exposed in a liquid. If ρwater = 1000 kg/m³, find the liquid density.
ρliquid = 1000 × (4 / 6) = 667 kg/m³

Surface Tension & Capillarity

Surface tension
γ = FL
WORKED EXAMPLE
A force of 0.03 N acts on a wire of length 5 cm floating on a liquid surface. Find the surface tension.
γ = 0.03 / 0.05 = 0.6 N/m
Excess pressure in a liquid drop
ΔP = 2γr
WORKED EXAMPLE
A water drop of radius 1 mm has surface tension 0.072 N/m. Find the excess pressure.
ΔP = (2 × 0.072) / 0.001 = 144 Pa
Excess pressure in a soap bubble
ΔP = 4γr
WORKED EXAMPLE
A soap bubble of radius 5 mm has surface tension 0.025 N/m. Find the excess pressure inside.
ΔP = (4 × 0.025) / 0.005 = 20 Pa
Capillary rise
h = 2γ cosθrρg
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water (γ = 0.072 N/m, θ = 0°) rises in a tube of radius 0.5 mm. Find the height of the column.
h = (2 × 0.072 × 1) / (0.0005 × 1000 × 9.8) = 0.144 / 4.9 = 2.94 cm
Capillary tube diameter
D = 4γ cosθρgh
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water rises 2.94 cm in a capillary tube (γ = 0.072 N/m, θ = 0°). Find the tube's internal diameter.
D = (4 × 0.072 × 1) / (1000 × 9.8 × 0.0294) = 0.288 / 288.12 = 1 mm

Hydrodynamics

Equation of continuity
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water flows at 2 m/s through a pipe of cross-section 0.01 m². Find the speed where the pipe narrows to 0.005 m².
V₂ = (0.01 × 2) / 0.005 = 4 m/s
Volume flow rate
Q = AV
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water flows through a pipe of area 0.002 m² at 3 m/s. Find the volume flow rate.
Q = 0.002 × 3 = 0.006 m³/s
Mass flow rate
ΔMΔt = ρAV
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³) flows through a pipe of area 0.002 m² at 3 m/s. Find the mass flow rate.
ΔM/Δt = 1000 × 0.002 × 3 = 6 kg/s
Bernoulli's equation
P + 12 ρV² + ρgh = constant
WORKED EXAMPLE
At point 1: P₁ = 200 kPa, V₁ = 2 m/s, h₁ = 5 m. At point 2: V₂ = 4 m/s, h₂ = 2 m. Find P₂ (ρ = 1000).
200k + 2k + 49k = P₂ + 8k + 19.6k → P₂ = 223.4 kPa
Stokes' law
F = 6πηrv
WORKED EXAMPLE
A sphere of radius 1 cm falls through a liquid (η = 0.001 Pa·s) at 0.5 m/s. Find the viscous drag.
F = 6π(0.001)(0.01)(0.5) ≈ 9.42 × 10⁻⁵ N
Terminal velocity
vt = 2r² (ρ − σ) g9η
WORKED EXAMPLE
A small sphere (r = 0.1 mm, ρ = 2000 kg/m³) falls through a liquid (σ = 1000 kg/m³, η = 0.001 Pa·s). Find its terminal velocity.
vt = (2 × 10⁻⁸ × 1000 × 9.8) / (9 × 0.001) ≈ 2.18 mm/s
Poiseuille's formula
Q = π r⁴ ΔP8ηL
WORKED EXAMPLE
A tube of radius 2 mm and length 0.5 m carries water (η = 0.001 Pa·s) under a pressure difference of 400 Pa. Find Q.
Q = (π × 1.6×10⁻¹¹ × 400) / (8 × 0.001 × 0.5) ≈ 5.03 × 10⁻⁶ m³/s
Reynolds number
R = 2Vrρη
WORKED EXAMPLE
Water (ρ = 1000, η = 0.001) flows at 0.1 m/s through a pipe of radius 5 mm. Determine the flow type.
R = (2 × 0.1 × 0.005 × 1000) / 0.001 = 1000 → Laminar flow
Flow classification
Laminar: R < 2000    Turbulent: R > 2000

Thermal Physics

Kelvin from Celsius
T(K) = θ(°C) + 273
WORKED EXAMPLE
Convert 25°C to Kelvin.
T = 25 + 273 = 298 K
Heat energy
Q = mcΔT
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the heat needed to raise 2 kg of water by 30°C (c = 4200 J/kg·K).
Q = 2 × 4200 × 30 = 252,000 J
Latent heat
Q = mL
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the heat needed to melt 0.5 kg of ice at 0°C (L = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg).
Q = 0.5 × 3.34×10⁵ = 167,000 J
First law of thermodynamics
Q = ΔU + W
WORKED EXAMPLE
A gas absorbs 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work. Find the change in internal energy.
ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200 = 300 J
Boyle's law
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A gas at 100 kPa occupies 2 L. If pressure rises to 200 kPa at constant temperature, find the new volume.
V₂ = (100 × 2) / 200 = 1 L
Charles' law
V₁T₁ = V₂T₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A gas occupies 2 L at 300 K. Find its volume at 450 K (constant pressure).
V₂ = (2 × 450) / 300 = 3 L
Gay-Lussac's law
P₁T₁ = P₂T₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
A gas at 100 kPa and 300 K is heated to 400 K at constant volume. Find the new pressure.
P₂ = (100 × 400) / 300 = 133.3 kPa
Combined gas law
P₁V₁T₁ = P₂V₂T₂
WORKED EXAMPLE
At 100 kPa, 2 L, 300 K, a gas is compressed to 1 L and heated to 400 K. Find the new pressure.
P₂ = (100 × 2 × 400) / (300 × 1) = 266.7 kPa
Ideal gas equation
PV = nRT = NkT
WORKED EXAMPLE
Find the volume occupied by 2 mol of ideal gas at 100 kPa and 300 K (R = 8.31 J/mol·K).
V = (2 × 8.31 × 300) / 100,000 ≈ 0.05 m³ = 50 L

Expansion of Solids & Liquids

Linear expansion
ΔL = α L ΔT
WORKED EXAMPLE
A 2 m steel rod (α = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ /K) is heated through 50 K. Find its expansion.
ΔL = 1.2×10⁻⁵ × 2 × 50 = 1.2 × 10⁻³ m = 1.2 mm
Area expansion
ΔA = β A ΔT
WORKED EXAMPLE
A metal plate of area 0.01 m² (β = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ /K) is heated through 100 K. Find its area increase.
ΔA = 2.4×10⁻⁵ × 0.01 × 100 = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ m²
Volume expansion
ΔV = γ V ΔT
WORKED EXAMPLE
A liquid of volume 1 L (γ = 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ /K) is heated through 40 K. Find its volume increase.
ΔV = 3.6×10⁻⁵ × 0.001 × 40 = 1.44 × 10⁻⁶ m³ = 1.44 mL
Relations between coefficients
β ≈ 2α    γ ≈ 3α
WORKED EXAMPLE
A solid has linear expansivity α = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ /K. Find its area and volume expansivities.
β = 2(1.8×10⁻⁵) = 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ /K, γ = 3(1.8×10⁻⁵) = 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ /K
Apparent cubic expansivity
γa = γr − γv
WORKED EXAMPLE
A liquid has real cubic expansivity 1.1 × 10⁻³ /K, contained in a vessel with cubic expansivity 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ /K. Find the apparent expansivity.
γa = 1.1×10⁻³ − 3.6×10⁻⁵ = 1.064 × 10⁻³ /K

Heat Transfer

Conduction
Qt = kA (ΔT)L
WORKED EXAMPLE
A copper rod (k = 400 W/m·K) of area 0.01 m² and length 0.5 m has ends at 100°C and 0°C. Find the heat flow rate.
Q/t = (400 × 0.01 × 100) / 0.5 = 800 W
Convection
Qt = hA (Ts − Tf)
WORKED EXAMPLE
A plate of area 2 m² at 80°C loses heat by convection to air at 20°C (h = 10 W/m²·K). Find the heat loss rate.
Q/t = 10 × 2 × (80 − 20) = 1200 W
Radiation (Stefan's law)
Qt = eσA T⁴
WORKED EXAMPLE
A black body (e = 0.8, A = 0.5 m²) at 500 K radiates energy. Find the power radiated (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²·K⁴).
Q/t = 0.8 × 5.67×10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × 500⁴ ≈ 1.42 kW
Net radiation
Qt = eσA (T₁⁴ − T₂⁴)
WORKED EXAMPLE
A body at 500 K radiates into surroundings at 300 K (e = 0.8, A = 0.5 m²). Find the net heat loss per second.
Q/t = 0.8 × 5.67×10⁻⁸ × 0.5 × (500⁴ − 300⁴) ≈ 1.23 kW
Stefan's constant
σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²·K⁴

Waves & Sound

Wave speed
v = fλ
WORKED EXAMPLE
A wave of frequency 500 Hz has wavelength 0.68 m. Find its speed.
v = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s
Frequency
f = 1T
WORKED EXAMPLE
A wave has a period of 0.02 s. Find its frequency.
f = 1 / 0.02 = 50 Hz
Open pipe (both ends open)
f = nV2L
WORKED EXAMPLE
An open pipe of length 0.5 m resonates in its fundamental mode. Find its frequency (V = 344 m/s).
f = (1 × 344) / (2 × 0.5) = 344 Hz
Closed pipe (one end closed)
f = (2n − 1) V4L
WORKED EXAMPLE
A closed pipe of length 0.5 m resonates in its fundamental. Find its frequency (V = 344 m/s).
f = (1 × 344) / (4 × 0.5) = 172 Hz
Echo distance
x = vt2
WORKED EXAMPLE
An echo is heard 2 s after a clap. If sound travels at 344 m/s, find the distance to the reflecting wall.
x = (344 × 2) / 2 = 344 m
Speed from echo
v = 2xt
WORKED EXAMPLE
An echo is heard 3 s after a sound is made towards a wall 510 m away. Find the speed of sound.
v = (2 × 510) / 3 = 340 m/s

Blood Pressure

Cardiac output
Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume
WORKED EXAMPLE
A person's heart beats 72 times per minute with a stroke volume of 70 mL. Find the cardiac output.
CO = 72 × 70 = 5040 mL/min ≈ 5 L/min
Pulse pressure
Pulse pressure = systolic − diastolic
WORKED EXAMPLE
A patient has blood pressure reading of 120/80 mmHg. Find the pulse pressure.
Pulse pressure = 120 − 80 = 40 mmHg
