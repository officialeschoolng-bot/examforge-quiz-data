Overview of the Cell

Overview of the Cell
  1. The cell is the fundamental, structural, and functional unit of all living organisms.
  2. Unicellular organisms exist independently as a single cell.
  3. Multicellular organisms are composed of many cells.
  4. Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus.
  5. Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  6. Cytoplasm is the semi-fluid matrix where cellular reactions occur.
  7. Organelles are membrane-bound structures found in eukaryotic cells.
  8. Ribosomes are non-membrane bound and found in all cells.
  9. Centrosomes are non-membrane bound and aid cell division in animal cells.
KEY POINT
The cell is the basic unit of life. Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotes possess one along with membrane-bound organelles.
Cell Size, Shape and Variation
  1. Mycoplasma is the smallest cell at 0.3 µm.
  2. Bacteria range from 3 to 5 micrometers.
  3. The ostrich egg is the largest isolated single cell.
  4. Human red blood cells are about 7.0 micrometers in diameter.
  5. Nerve cells are some of the longest cells.
  6. Shapes vary widely: disc-like, polygonal, columnar, cuboid, thread-like, or irregular.
  7. Cell shape often relates to its function.

Organelles Common to Plant & Animal Cells

Common Organelles
Nucleus: Largest organelle. Enclosed by a nuclear envelope with pores. Contains nucleoplasm and chromatin. Nucleolus synthesizes rRNA.
Mitochondrion: Double membrane with inner folds called cristae. Powerhouse of the cell. Produces ATP via cellular respiration.
Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): Network of tubules and cisternae. Rough ER (with ribosomes) synthesizes proteins. Smooth ER (no ribosomes) synthesizes lipids and detoxifies.
Golgi Apparatus: Stack of flattened cisternae. Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids. Forms lysosomes.
Ribosomes: Composed of rRNA and protein. Eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S. Site of protein synthesis.
Lysosomes: Single membrane. Contain hydrolytic enzymes that work at an acidic pH of 5. Digests macromolecules and recycles cellular components.
Peroxisomes: Single membrane. Contain catalase to break down hydrogen peroxide. Oxidize fatty acids.
Cytoskeleton: Network of protein filaments including microfilaments, intermediate filaments, and microtubules. Provides mechanical support and enables movement.
Cell Membrane: Phospholipid bilayer embedded with proteins. Fluid mosaic model. Regulates molecular traffic in and out of the cell.

Unique Organelles

Organelles Unique to Plant Cells
  1. Chloroplasts: Double membrane. Contains stroma, thylakoids, and grana. Site of photosynthesis.
  2. Cell Wall: Rigid structure outside the plasma membrane. Made primarily of cellulose. Provides structural support and protection.
  3. Central Vacuole: Occupies 30 to 90 percent of cell volume. Surrounded by the tonoplast. Maintains turgor pressure and stores nutrients.
Organelles Unique to Animal Cells
  1. Centrioles and Centrosome: Cylindrical structures made of microtubule triplets. Organize the mitotic spindle during cell division.
  2. Lysosomes: Significantly more numerous and prominent than in plant cells. Crucial for immune defense and tissue remodeling.

Comparison of Plant & Animal Cells

Plant vs Animal Cells
Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell Wall Present, made of cellulose. Absent.
Shape Fixed, rectangular or box-shaped. Irregular, rounded, or variable.
Size Generally larger (10 to 100 micrometers). Generally smaller (10 to 30 micrometers).
Chloroplasts Present. Absent.
Vacuoles One large central vacuole. Small, numerous, or absent.
Centrioles Absent in most higher plants. Present.
Lysosomes Rare or absent. Numerous and prominent.
Nutrition Mode Autotrophic. Heterotrophic.
Carbohydrate Storage Starch. Glycogen.
