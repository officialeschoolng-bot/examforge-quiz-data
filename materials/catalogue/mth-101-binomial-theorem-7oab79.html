BINOMIAL THEOREM
Expansion, Pascal's Triangle, and Specific Terms

What is a "BINOMIAL"?

Definition
First things first: "Bi" means two, "nomial" means term. So a binomial is just an expression with two terms separated by a plus or minus sign.
Examples of BINOMIALS:
  • x + 3
  • 2a - 5b
  • 3x - 7y

The Problem: Expanding Binomials

Manual Expansion
If we wish to expand a binomial of the form (x + y)n, when the exponent is small, we can multiply everything out manually.
EXAMPLE 1 — EXPAND (x + y)²
This means (x + y)(x + y). We use FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last):

• First: x × x = x2
• Outer: x × y = xy
• Inner: y × x = xy
• Last: y × y = y2

Combine like terms: x2 + 2xy + y2
Solution: (x + y)2 = x2 + 2xy + y2
EXAMPLE 2 — EXPAND (x + y)³
This is (x + y)(x + y)(x + y). Let's do it step by step:

First, expand (x + y)2 as we did: x2 + 2xy + y2

Now multiply by (x + y):
= (x2 + 2xy + y2)(x + y)
= x3 + x2y + 2x2y + 2xy2 + xy2 + y3
= x3 + 3x2y + 3xy2 + y3

Solution: (x + y)3 = x3 + 3x2y + 3xy2 + y3
Here's the PROBLEM
What if we need to expand (x + y)7 or (x + y)10? Doing this by FOIL would take forever! We need a better method.

Discovering a Pattern: Pascal's Triangle

The Pattern
Look at the coefficients we've found so far:
  • 1
  • 1x + 1y
  • 1x2 + 2xy + 1y2
  • 1x3 + 3x2y + 3xy2 + 1y3
Let's arrange these coefficients in a triangle (Pascal's Triangle):
(x + y)0            1
(x + y)1          1   1
(x + y)2       1   2   1
(x + y)3    1   3   3   1
(x + y)4  1   4   6   4   1
(x + y)5 1   5   10   10   5   1
(x + y)6 1   6   15   20   15   6   1
How Pascal's Triangle works (How to draw it from memory):
  1. Start with 1 at the top.
  2. Each number is the sum of the two numbers directly above it.
How to Use It
Magic: The row corresponding to exponent n gives us the coefficients for (x + y)n!
Notice that when the exponent is 0, we get one term. When the exponent is 1, we get two terms. When the exponent is 2, we get three terms. When the exponent is 3, we get four terms.
If the exponent is n, after expanding, we will have n + 1 terms.
EXAMPLE — EXPAND (x + y)⁴ USING PASCAL'S TRIANGLE
Find row for exponent 4 from Pascal's triangle: 1, 4, 6, 4, 1

Now write the expansion:
  • Start with x4, then decrease x's exponent by 1 each term.
  • Start with y0 (which is 1), then increase y's exponent by 1 each term.
(x + y)4 = 1x4y0 + 4x3y1 + 6x2y2 + 4x1y3 + 1x0y4

Simplify: x4 + 4x3y + 6x2y2 + 4xy3 + y4

Much faster than FOIL!

Finding Specific Terms (The Exam Favorite!)

The Exam Favorite
Exams RARELY ask you to expand the whole thing. They ask things like:
  • "Find the 3rd term in the expansion of (2x - 3)5"
  • "What is the coefficient of x3 in (x + 2)7"
Method 1: Using Pascal's Triangle (for small exponents)
  1. Find Pascal's row for exponent 5: 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1
  2. The 4th term uses the 4th coefficient: 10
The pattern for (a + b)5:
  • 1st term: a5b0
  • 2nd term: a4b1
  • 3rd term: a3b2
  • 4th term: a2b3
We stop at the 4th term since we were asked for the 4th term.
NB: a's exponent + b's exponent = n (in this case, 5)


So the 4th term is: 10a2b3

The Binomial Theorem Formula (For Any Exponent!)

The General Formula
What if we need (x + y)100? Pascal's Triangle would be HUGE! We need a formula.
For any positive integer n:
(x + y)n = nC0 · xn · y0 + nC1 · xn-1 · y1 + nC2 · xn-2 · y2 + ... + nCn · x0 · yn
Or with summation notation:
(x + y)n = ∑ nCk xn-k yk
If you can't use the Sigma (Summation) notation, it's alright, you don't necessarily need to understand the Summation notation to solve BINOMIAL EXPANSION.
What is nCk? (The Combination Formula)
nCk = n!k!(n - k)!
This is the binomial coefficient, read as "n combination k".
Where ! means factorial:
5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120
3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
0! = 1 (by definition)
EXAMPLE — CALCULATING 5C2
5C2 = 5! / [2! · (5 - 2)!] = 5! / (2! · 3!)
= (5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1) / [(2 × 1) × (3 × 2 × 1)]
= 120 / (2 × 6) = 120 / 12 = 10

Look familiar? That's the same as the 3rd number in row 5 of Pascal's Triangle!

Notice how when we found 5 Combination 2 (5C2), it gave the same value as the (2 + 1)th term of the Pascal's triangle.

Finding Specific Terms Quickly (The Formula Method)

The (k+1)th Term Formula
This is the MOST POWERFUL technique for exams! For the expansion of (a + b)n:
Tk+1 = nCk · an-k · bk
Important: If you want the kth term, use k - 1 in the formula.
Formula Examples
EXAMPLE 1 — FIND THE 4TH TERM OF (2x - 3)⁵
Here: n = 5 (the power). We want the 4th term, so k = 3 (because k + 1 = 4).
a = 2x, b = -3.

Plug into formula:
T4 = 5C3 · (2x)5-3 · (-3)3
T4 = 5C3 · (2x)2 · (-27)

Calculate:
• 5C3 = 5C2 = 10
• (2x)2 = 4x2
• (-27) from (-3)3

Multiply: 10 × 4x2 × (-27) = 10 × (-108x2) = -1080x2

Answer: The 4th term is -1080x2.
EXAMPLE 2 — FIND THE TERM CONTAINING x³ IN (x + 2)⁷
Here: n = 7, a = x, b = 2.

We want the exponent of x to be 3.
Since a = x, in the term nCk · an-k · bk, the exponent on a is n - k.
So: n - k = 3 → 7 - k = 3 → k = 4.

Now use the formula:
T4+1 = T5 = 7C4 · x3 · 24

Calculate:
• 7C4 = 7! / (4! · 3!) = (7 × 6 × 5) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 35
• 24 = 16

Multiply: 35 · x3 · 16 = 560x3

Quick Practice Problem (Try It!)

Practice Problem
Problem: Find the constant term (term with no x) in the expansion of (2x2 - 1/x)6.

Think about: We have (2x2)6-k · (-1/x)k. We want the x's to cancel out.
Tap to reveal step-by-step solution
1. Write the general term:
Tk+1 = 6Ck · (2x2)6-k · (-1/x)k

2. Simplify the x part:
(x2)6-k = x12-2k and (1/x)k = x-k
So total x exponent: 12 - 2k - k = 12 - 3k

3. We want constant term:
→ exponent of x = 0
So: 12 - 3k = 0 → 3k = 12 → k = 4

4. Now find the term:
T4+1 = T5 = 6C4 · (2x2)2 · (-1/x)4
• 6C4 = 15
• (2x2)2 = 4x4
• (-1/x)4 = 1/x4 (positive since even power)

5. Multiply:
15 × 4x4 × (1/x4) = 15 × 4 × 1 = 60

Answer: The constant term is 60.

The Four Scenarios

These FOUR scenarios are the ways you'll most likely be seeing questions.
SCENARIO 1: Finding rth term or (k + 1)th term
EXAMPLE 1 — 3RD TERM OF (3x + 2)⁵
Quick solution (in seconds):
Formula for the (k+1)th term: Tk+1 = nCk · an-k · bk

Here, n = 5, k = 3 - 1 = 2.
a = 3x, b = 2.

Plug in and compute mentally:
• C(5,2) = 10
• (3x)5-2 = (3x)3 = 27x3
• 22 = 4

Multiply: 10 × 27x3 × 4 = 1080x3.

Result: The 3rd term is 1080x3.

Why it's fast: You skip writing the general term; directly use k = r - 1, where r is the term we are looking for. If we are looking for the fifth term take k as 4 (i.e., 5 - 1). Then compute the three parts (binomial coefficient, power of a, power of b) then multiply.

NB: C(5, 2) is another way of writing 5C2.
EXAMPLE 2 — 5TH TERM OF (3x - 2y)⁸
Formula for the (k+1)th term: Tk+1 = nCk · an-k · bk

Here, n = 8, k = 5 - 1 = 4.
a = 3x, b = -2y.

Plug in and compute mentally:
• 8C4 = 70
• (3x)8-4 = (3x)4 = 81x4
• (-2y)4 = 16y4

Multiply: 70 × 81x4 × 16y4 = 90720x4y4.

Result: The 5th term is 90720x4y4.
SCENARIO 2: Finding the Coefficient of a Term
EXAMPLE 1 — COEFFICIENT OF x⁵ IN (2x - 3)⁸
Step-by-step solution:

1. Identify n, a, and b: n = 8, a = 2x, b = -3.

2. Write the general term Tk+1:
Tk+1 = 8Ck · (2x)8-k · (-3)k

3. Simplify the power of x:
Tk+1 = 8Ck · 28-k · x8-k · (-3)k

4. We want the exponent of x to be 5, so set 8 - k = 5. This gives k = 3.

5. Check: k = 3 is an integer between 0 and 8, so the term exists.

6. Find the coefficient by plugging k = 3 into the expression:
Term = 8C3 · (2x)5 · (-3)3

Calculate:
• 8C3 = 56
• 25 = 32
• (-3)3 = -27

Multiply: 56 × 32x5 = 1792x5
Then 1792x5 × (-27) = -48384x5.

Answer: The coefficient of x5 is -48384.
EXAMPLE 2 — COEFFICIENT OF x⁶y² IN (3x - 2y)⁸
Step-by-step solution:

1. Identify n, a, and b: n = 8, a = 3x, b = -2y.

2. Write the general term Tk+1:
Tk+1 = 8Ck · (3x)8-k · (-2y)k

3. We want the exponent of x to be 6, so set 8 - k = 6. This gives k = 2.
Or We want the exponent of y to be 2, so set k = 2.

4. Check: k = 2 is an integer between 0 and 8, so the term exists.

5. Find the coefficient by plugging k = 2 into the expression:
Term = 8C2 · (3x)8-2 · (-2y)2

Calculate:
• 8C2 = 28
• (3x)6 = 729x6
• (-2y)2 = 4y2

Multiply: 28 × 729x6 = 20412x6
Then 20412x6 × (4y2) = 81648x6y2.

Answer: The coefficient of x6y2 is 81648.
SCENARIO 3: Finding the Constant Term
EXAMPLE 1 — CONSTANT TERM IN (2x - 1/x)⁸
General term:
Tk+1 = nCk · (2x)8-k · (-1/x)k

Which is the same as:
Tk+1 = nCk · (2x)8-k · (-1x)-k

For constant term, power of x = 0
So: x8-k × x-k = x0
which is x8-k-k = x0

Since the bases are the same, equate the power:
8 - k - k = 0
8 - 2k = 0
k = 4

Now substitute k = 4:
Tk+1 = nCk · (2x)8-k · (-1/x)k
T5 = 8C4 × (2x)4 × (-1/x)4

Calculate:
• 8C4 = 70
• (2x)4 = 16x4
• (-1/x)4 = 1/x4

Constant term = 70 × 16x4 × 1/x4 = 1120

Notice how x4 cancelled out.

If the k is bigger than the exponent (8) or smaller than 0 (maybe a negative number), then the term doesn't exist or, in this case there's no constant term. CONSTANT TERM IS 0.
SCENARIO 4: Finding Middle Term
EXAMPLE 1 — MIDDLE TERM IN (x + y)⁸
Since the power is 8, there will be 9 terms after expanding. The middle term is the 5th term.
EXAMPLE 2 — MIDDLE TERM IN (x + y)⁷
Since the power is 7, there will be 8 terms after expanding. The middle term is the 4th and 5th terms.

Summary Cheat Sheet

Key Takeaways
  1. For small n: Use Pascal's Triangle.
  2. For any n: Use Binomial Theorem: (a + b)n = ∑ [nCk · an-k · bk].
  3. Specific term: Use Tk+1 = nCk · an-k · bk.
    • Remember: k starts at 0 for the first term!
  4. Watch for signs: If b is negative, bk alternates signs.
  5. For fractional/negative exponents: The theorem works differently (that's for another video!).
The key is PRACTICE. Start with Pascal's Triangle for small exponents to build intuition, then move to the formula for larger ones. You've got this!
Final Tip: On exams, if you forget the formula, you can always generate Pascal's Triangle quickly for n ≤ 6 or 7. It's a great backup plan!
