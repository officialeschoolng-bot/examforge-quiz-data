BIO 101 — Introduction to Biology
Definitions, classifications, and characteristics

Definition & Branches

Definition of Biology
  1. Biology is the study of living organisms.
  2. Covers origins, anatomy, morphology, physiology, behaviour, and distribution.
  3. Derived from Greek bios meaning life and logos meaning study.
  4. Defined as the science of life and living organisms.
KEY POINT
Biology is the science of life and living organisms.
Branches of Biology
Anatomy: Study of organisms' structures.
Biochemistry: Chemical reactions required for life, at cellular level.
Bioinformatics: IT for study, collection, and storage of genomic data.
Biotechnology: Manipulation of living matter.
Botany: Study of plants.
Cell biology: Study of the cell and internal molecular/chemical interactions.
Ecology: Interactions between organisms and their non-living environment.
Genetics: Study of genes and heredity.
Immunology: Study of the immune system.
Microbiology: Study of microscopic organisms and their interactions.
Molecular biology: Biological functions at the molecular level.
Pathology: Study of diseases, causes, and development.
Pharmacology: Interactions between drugs and organisms.
Physiology: Functions and mechanisms in living organisms.
Zoology: Study of animals (classification, physiology, development, behaviour).

Living Things & Classification

Living Things
  1. Refers to any organism possessing the characteristics of life.
  2. Includes plants, animals, and microorganisms.
General Classification of Living Things
  1. Living things were initially grouped as plants or animals.
  2. Both are eukaryotic.
  3. Animals: Motile and heterotrophic.
  4. Plants: Non-motile, photosynthetic, and have a cell wall.
  5. Bacteria and Archaea: Prokaryotes (lack a nucleus). Single-celled with cell walls made of different materials.
Taxonomic Hierarchy
  1. Kingdom
  2. Phylum
  3. Class
  4. Order
  5. Family
  6. Genus
  7. Species
Classification Levels
Kingdom: The most fundamental classification level. Five kingdoms exist: Animalia, Plantae, Monera, Protista, Fungi.
Phylum: Groups organisms based on physical resemblance and common ancestry.
Class: Subdivides organisms of a phylum. Organisms in a class share more traits than those in the entire phylum (e.g., mammals).
Order: Subdivides classes. A taxonomy key (checklist of characteristics) is used for grouping.
Family: Subdivides orders. Organisms within a family are closely related.
Genus: Highly specific classification. Used to complete the first part of an organism's two-part scientific name.
Species: The lowest and most exacting level. Defined by the ability to breed with other organisms of the same species. Regulates the second part of the two-part scientific name.

Energy & Carbon Utilization

Classification Based on Energy Utilization
Autotrophs: Produce their own energy-rich organic compounds using inorganic sources. Utilize sunlight (photosynthesis) or inorganic compounds (chemosynthesis). Convert carbon dioxide into organic molecules. Examples: Plants, algae, some bacteria.
Heterotrophs: Obtain energy by consuming other organic matter. Rely on autotrophs or other heterotrophs for energy. Examples: Animals, fungi, most bacteria.
Classification on the Basis of Carbon Utilization
Photoautotrophs: Use sunlight as an energy source. Convert carbon dioxide into organic molecules via photosynthesis. Examples: Plants, algae, some bacteria.
Chemoautotrophs: Use energy from chemical reactions (oxidizing inorganic compounds). Produce organic molecules. Examples: Certain bacteria and archaea in extreme environments.
Photoheterotrophs: Obtain energy from sunlight. Rely on organic compounds as a carbon source. Combine photosynthesis with organic uptake. Examples: Some bacteria and protists.
Chemoheterotrophs: Obtain both energy and carbon by consuming organic compounds. Examples: Animals, fungi, most bacteria, many protists.
