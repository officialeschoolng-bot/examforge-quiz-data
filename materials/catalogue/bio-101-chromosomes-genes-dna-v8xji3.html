BIO 101 — Chromosomes, Genes & DNA
Structure, expression, inheritance, and abnormalities

Chromosomes

Chromosomes
Chromosomes are structures in the nucleus made of DNA and proteins.
They carry the genetic information that determines an organism's characteristics.
They become highly condensed and visible under a microscope during cell division.
This condensation protects DNA from damage and packages it efficiently.
During cell division, chromosomes replicate so each daughter cell receives an identical copy.
Chromosomes help regulate gene expression through regulatory regions or promoters.
KEY POINT
Chromosomes = DNA + protein. They condense during cell division to protect and package DNA, and replicate so daughter cells receive identical copies.
Chromatin and Centromere
Chromatin is the combination of DNA and proteins (histones) making up the genetic material.
It is less condensed during interphase and highly condensed during cell division.
The centromere is the specialized region where spindle fibers attach during mitosis and meiosis.
Types of chromosomes based on centromere placement include:
  1. Metacentric
  2. Submetacentric
  3. Acrocentric
  4. Telocentric
  5. Holocentric

Genes

Genes
Genes are segments of DNA containing instructions for building proteins.
They are the fundamental units of heredity and transmit traits across generations.
The location of a gene on a chromosome is called its locus.
The specific arrangement of genes reflects the evolutionary history of an organism.
Structure of Genes
Exons: Coding regions containing instructions for building proteins.
Introns: Non-coding regions that are removed during gene expression.
Promoters: DNA sequences located upstream that initiate transcription.
Enhancers: DNA sequences located far away that help regulate gene expression.
Gene Expression
Gene expression is the process of turning genes into physical traits.
Transcription copies the DNA sequence of a gene into mRNA.
The mRNA travels outside the nucleus to ribosomes in the cytoplasm.
Translation converts the mRNA sequence into a specific sequence of amino acids.
The amino acid sequence determines the protein's structure and function.
KEY POINT
Central Dogma: DNA → mRNA (transcription) → protein (translation).

DNA

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)
DNA is the molecule containing genetic instructions for cellular processes.
It is the genetic material making up chromosomes.
DNA is composed of four nucleotide bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G).
The order of these bases forms the genetic code.
Relationship Between Chromosomes and Genes
Genes are located on specific positions (loci) on chromosomes.
Each chromosome carries multiple genes, influencing an organism's characteristics.
During reproduction, genes from each parent exchange information through recombination.
This genetic recombination introduces variation and diversity into offspring.
Genes located on the same chromosome are linked and tend to be inherited together.

Sex Chromosomes & Abnormalities

Sex Chromosomes
Females typically have two X chromosomes (XX), while males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY).
The Y chromosome triggers the development of male characteristics.
The SRY gene on the Y chromosome initiates the development of male gonads.
Absence of the Y chromosome and SRY gene leads to the development of female gonads.
KEY POINT
Sex determination: XX = female, XY = male. The SRY gene on the Y chromosome is the male-determining switch.
Chromosomal Abnormalities
Occur when there are changes or errors in the structure or number of chromosomes.
These changes can disrupt normal protein production and lead to health issues.
Examples include:
  1. Down syndrome: Caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21.
  2. Turner syndrome: Caused by a missing or incomplete X chromosome in females.
  3. Klinefelter syndrome: Caused by abnormalities in the sex chromosomes.
