1. The Wave Nature of Electrons

The observation of hydrogen spectra explained the dual nature of the electron, showing it behaves as both a wave and a particle. When light is dispersed by a prism, a spectrum is produced.

c = λ × ν
c Velocity of light
3 × 10⁸ m/s
λ Wavelength
(mµ or Å)
ν (Frequency) Reciprocal of wavelength (1/λ), measured in cm⁻¹

Quick Check 1

If the frequency of a wave increases, what happens to its wavelength (assuming velocity is constant)?
Because c = λ × ν, if c is constant and ν increases, λ must decrease to maintain the equation.

Types of Spectra

Solid Substances (Continuous)

When solid substances are heated, they produce continuous line spectra.

Gaseous Substances (Line/Band)

Gaseous substances give sharp line or band spectra shown as discrete, separated parallel lines.

The Hydrogen Spectrum

The hydrogen atom has one electron and one proton. When excited by an electric discharge, it emits light at a series of frequencies. Balmer pointed out that the wavelength of light in the visible region fit an expression:

1/λ = R × (1/2² − 1/n²)

where n = 3, 4, 5, … and R is the Rydberg constant with a value of 1.09679 × 10⁵ cm⁻¹.

Rydberg contributed the general formula:

1/λ = V̄ = R × (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)

Quick Check 2

In the Ritz equation, what does the symbol V̄ (V-bar) represent?
V̄ (V-bar) is the wave number, which is equal to 1/λ (the reciprocal of wavelength).

The Hydrogen Series

Series Region Equation n₂ Values
Lyman UV V̄ = R(1/1² − 1/n₂²) 2, 3, 4, …
Balmer Visible V̄ = R(1/2² − 1/n₂²) 3, 4, 5, …
Paschen IR V̄ = R(1/3² − 1/n₂²) 4, 5, 6, …
Brackett IR V̄ = R(1/4² − 1/n₂²) 5, 6, 7, …
Pfund IR V̄ = R(1/5² − 1/n₂²) 6, 7, 8, …

Worked Example 1

Balmer → Brackett Transition (R = 109737 cm⁻¹)

1
Apply Ritz equation:
V̄ = 109737 × (1/2² − 1/4²)
V̄ = 109737 × (1/4 − 1/16) = 109737 × (3/16)
V̄ = 2.06 × 10⁴ cm⁻¹
2
Calculate wavelength:
λ = 1 / V̄ = 1 / (2.06 × 10⁴)
λ = 4.86 × 10⁻⁵ cm

Worked Example 2

First Lyman Line: n₂ = 2 → n₁ = 1 (R = 1.097 × 10⁵ cm⁻¹)

1
Ritz equation:
V̄ = 1.097 × 10⁵ × (1/1² − 1/2²)
V̄ = 1.097 × 10⁵ × (1 − 1/4) = 1.097 × 10⁵ × (3/4)
V̄ = 8.2275 × 10⁴ cm⁻¹
2
Wavelength:
λ = 1 / V̄ = 1 / (8.2275 × 10⁴)
λ = 1.215 × 10⁻⁵ cm

Gravimetric Laws

Gravimetry is an analytical method that involves weight measurement as a way of determining the constituents of any substance.

There are two basic gravimetric laws:

1. Law of Conservation of Mass Matter can neither be created nor destroyed. The mass of reactants equals the mass of products.
2. Law of Definite Proportion All pure samples of a compound contain similar elements combined in the same proportion by mass.

Quick Check 3

In the reaction BaCl₂ + H₂SO₄ → BaSO₄ + 2HCl, which law guarantees that the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products?
Lavoisier's Law of Conservation of Mass states that in an enclosed system, the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

Inexactness of Laws

The Law of Conservation of Mass is not exactly perfect. In some nuclear reactions, energy is absorbed or evolved, leading to mass destruction or creation.

E = Δm × c²

Where E is energy, Δm is the mass created or destroyed, and c is the velocity of light. When energy is evolved (e.g., radioactivity), mass is destroyed.

The Law of Definite Proportion is also invalidated by metals with variable oxidation states (e.g., Cu₂O vs CuO, or FeS vs Fe₂O₃).

CaCO₃ Decomposition

10 g of CaCO₃ heated to give 5.6 g of CaO and some CO₂. Density of CO₂ = 1.97 g/L.

1
Conservation of mass:
Mass of CaCO₃ = Mass of CaO + Mass of CO₂
10 g = 5.6 g + Mass of CO₂
Mass of CO₂ = 4.4 g
2
Volume using density:
Volume = Mass / Density = 4.4 / 1.97
Volume = 2.23 L

Verifying Conservation

5 g of MCO₃ heated to leave 2.8 g of MO. Molar mass of M = 40 g/mol.

1
Conservation:
5 g = 2.8 g + Mass of CO₂
Mass of CO₂ = 2.2 g
2
Stoichiometric verification:
Molar mass MCO₃ = 100 g/mol
Moles MCO₃ = 5 / 100 = 0.05 mol
Theoretical mass CO₂ = 0.05 × 44 = 2.2 g
✓ Law Verified

Key Takeaways

  • The wave motion of electrons is defined by velocity, wavelength, and frequency (c = λ × ν).
  • The hydrogen spectrum consists of distinct series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund).
  • The Ritz equation calculates the wave number of spectral lines.
  • The Law of Conservation of Mass states matter is not created or destroyed in reactions.
  • Mass defect in nuclear reactions (E = Δm × c²) is an exception to classical gravimetry.
  • Variable oxidation states invalidate the Law of Definite Proportion.
