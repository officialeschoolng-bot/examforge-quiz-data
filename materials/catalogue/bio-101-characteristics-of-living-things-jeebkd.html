BIO 101 — Characteristics of Living Things
General characteristics and the seven key traits

General Characteristics

General Characteristics of Living Things
  1. Made out of cells (tiny building blocks of life).
  2. Have DNA to carry genetic code passed from generation to generation.
  3. Able to grow and reproduce.
  4. Respond to stimuli or the environment.
  5. Adapt over time.
  6. Make their own energy or eat food to gain energy.
  7. Maintain internal balance (homeostasis).
KEY POINT
All living things share common features: cellular organization, genetic material (DNA), growth, reproduction, response to stimuli, adaptation, energy utilization, and homeostasis.

Seven Key Traits of Living Things

Seven Key Traits
  1. Homeostasis
  2. Organization
  3. Metabolism
  4. Growth and Development
  5. Adaptation
  6. Response to stimuli
  7. Reproduction
Homeostasis
  1. Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment.
  2. Internal conditions remain steady regardless of external conditions.
  3. Example: Humans maintain stable body temperature by shivering in the cold.
Organization
  1. Multiple levels of organization.
  2. Molecules are organized into one or more cells.
  3. The cell is the basic unit of structure and function.
  4. Living things appear different outside but cells are very similar.
Metabolism
  1. Use of energy by cells.
  2. Building up of chemical compounds is called anabolism.
  3. Breaking down of organic matter is called catabolism.
  4. Energy is required for homeostasis, growth, and other life processes.
Growth
  1. Increase in size.
  2. Occurs when the rate of anabolism is higher than catabolism.
  3. Example: An infant changing dramatically in size by adulthood.
Adaptations
  1. A characteristic of populations, not individuals.
  2. Individuals carry a variety of genes.
  3. Some individuals withstand environmental changes and reproduce more.
  4. Results in a change in allele frequencies over time.
  5. Comes about through the process of natural selection.
Response to Stimuli
  1. Detection of changes in the environment and responding to them.
  2. Movement of a unicellular organism to external chemicals is called chemotaxis.
  3. Leaves of a plant turning toward the sun is called phototropism.
  4. A response is often expressed by motion.
Reproduction
  1. Process by which living things give rise to offspring.
  2. Can be as simple as a single cell dividing into two (bacteria).
  3. Can be complex, as in human beings.
  4. Capable of passing on genetic information to ensure species survival.
