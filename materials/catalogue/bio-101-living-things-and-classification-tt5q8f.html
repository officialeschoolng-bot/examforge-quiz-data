BIO 101 — Living Things & Classification
Kingdoms, taxonomy, and nutritional types

Living Things & General Classification

What Are Living Things
  1. Any organism or living form possessing the characteristics of life.
  2. Includes plants, animals, and microorganisms.
General Classification of Living Things
  1. Initially classified as plants or animals.
  2. Both are eukaryotic but differ in nutrition and cellular features.
  3. Animals: Motile and heterotrophic.
  4. Plants: Non-motile, photosynthetic, and have a cell wall.
  5. Bacteria and Archaea: Prokaryotes, single-celled, different cell wall materials.
  6. Taxonomy levels: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species.

Kingdoms

Kingdoms
  1. The most fundamental classification level.
  2. Five kingdoms exist.
Characteristics of the Five Kingdoms
Kingdom Description
Animalia Multicellular, consume other organisms.
Plantae Multicellular, produce food via photosynthesis.
Monera Single-celled, no nucleus (bacteria, blue-green algae).
Protista Mostly single-celled (amoebas, algae).
Fungi Mushrooms, molds, yeast.

Taxonomic Levels (Phylum → Species)

Phylum
  1. Based on shared structural features, genetics, and development.
  2. Arthropoda: Jointed legs and exoskeleton.
  3. Chordata: Notochord at some life stage (vertebrates).
  4. Mollusca: Soft-bodied, often with shells.
  5. Tracheophyta: Vascular plants.
Classes
  1. Further subdivides a phylum.
  2. Organisms share more traits than in the entire phylum.
  3. Chordata classes: Mammalia, Aves, Reptilia, Amphibia, Osteichthyes, Chondrichthyes.
  4. Mammalia includes humans, dogs, and whales.
Order
  1. Further subdivides classes.
  2. Taxonomy key (checklist of characteristics) used for grouping.
Families
  1. Further subdivides orders.
  2. Organisms within a family are closely related.
  3. Animal examples: Felidae (cats), Canidae (dogs), Ursidae (bears).
  4. Plant example: Solanaceae (potatoes, tomatoes).
Genus
  1. Very specific classification.
  2. Used for the first part of the two-part scientific name.
Species
  1. Lowest and most exacting level.
  2. Defined by the ability to breed with others of the same species.
  3. Used for the second part of the two-part scientific name.
  4. Examples: Homo sapiens (humans), Felis catus (domestic cats), Canis lupus familiaris (dogs).
KEY POINT
Binomial nomenclature: Genus (capitalised) + species (lowercase), both italicised — e.g. Homo sapiens.

Energy & Carbon Utilization

Classification Based on Energy Utilization
  1. Based on source of energy and source of carbon.
  2. Autotrophs: Produce own energy-rich compounds from inorganic sources.
  3. Heterotrophs: Consume other organic matter for energy.
Autotrophs
  1. Convert carbon dioxide into organic molecules.
  2. Examples: Plants, algae, some bacteria.
Heterotrophs
  1. Rely on consuming autotrophs or other heterotrophs.
  2. Examples: Animals, fungi, most bacteria.
Types of Autotrophs
  1. Photoautotrophs: Use sunlight for photosynthesis.
  2. Chemoautotrophs: Use energy from chemical reactions (e.g., in extreme environments).
Types of Heterotrophs
  1. Photoheterotrophs: Obtain energy from sunlight, rely on organic compounds for carbon.
  2. Chemoheterotrophs: Obtain both energy and carbon from consuming organic compounds.
