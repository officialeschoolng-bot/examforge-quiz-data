BIO 101 — General Reproduction in Living Things
Asexual, sexual, and modes of animal reproduction

Overview & Types

Overview
Reproduction is a biological process where living organisms produce new organisms.
It maintains continuity of life and prevents species extinction.
It ensures the continuity of genes over generations and introduces new genes.
KEY POINT
Reproduction ensures continuity of life, continuity of genes, and introduces genetic variation.
Types of Reproduction
  1. Asexual reproduction: A single individual produces copies of itself without genetic contribution.
  2. Sexual reproduction: A new organism is created by combining genetic material from two parents.

Asexual Reproduction

Asexual Reproduction
A single parent gives rise to a new individual.
No gametes or genetic materials are formed.
The resulting organism is an identical clone of its parent.
Multicellular asexual organisms inherit genes from a single parent.
Types of Asexual Reproduction
1. Fission: The parent cell divides, and resultant cells regenerate into individuals.
a. Binary Fission: Parent cell divides into two cells under favorable conditions. Genetic material duplicates before division.
b. Multiple Fission: Single parent cell produces multiple copies. Occurs under unfavorable conditions, such as in algae.
2. Budding: New organisms form from outgrowths on the parent's body. They detach on maturity, leaving scar tissue (e.g., yeast, hydra).
3. Vegetative Propagation: New plants grow from fragments or special structures of the parent. Reproducing plants give rise to clones. Can be natural (runners, bulbs) or artificial (grafting, cutting).
4. Spore Formation: Organisms form dormant or reproductive spores under unfavorable conditions. Reproductive spores develop into new individuals without fusion (e.g., fungi, protozoa).
5. Regeneration: A part of the parent develops into a new individual. Specialized cells proliferate to form tissues (e.g., starfish, planaria).

Sexual Reproduction

Sexual Reproduction
Fusion of gametes from two different parents gives rise to a new individual.
Mixing of genetic material occurs, resulting in a mixed genome.
This introduces genetic variation at the genetic level.
Sexual Reproduction in Plants
Flowering plants (angiosperms) reproduce sexually.
The flower is the reproductive organ containing male gametes (pollen grains) and female gametes (egg).
Gametogenesis is the process where pollen travels from anther to stigma (pollination).
Fusion of gametes leads to fertilization, forming a zygote.
The zygote develops into an embryo, which turns into seeds inside fruits.
KEY POINT
Plant sexual sequence: Pollination → Fertilization → Zygote → Embryo → Seed (inside fruit).
Sexual Reproduction in Animals
Testes form sperms and ovaries form eggs.
Sperms fertilize the egg to form a zygote, which develops into an embryo.
The offspring inherits one allele for each trait from each parent.
The mixing of genes is the key to evolution.

Modes of Reproduction in Animals

Oviparity, Viviparity & Ovoviviparity
Mode Description Examples
Oviparity Birth of young ones from laid eggs. Fertilization may be external or internal. Birds, fishes, reptiles.
Viviparity Embryo grows inside the maternal body until capable of independent existence. Derives nutrition via the placenta. Humans, whales.
Ovoviviparity Embryo remains inside an egg within the mother's body until ready to hatch. Sharks, rays, snakes.
